About Bibliofeed

A feed where every post is a Bible verse, chosen at random from the whole Bible. No algorithm, no ads, no tracking. Scroll, read the chapter around a verse, keep the ones that stay with you, and, if you like, share your notes with friends.

How it works

Each post is one verse. Swipe the card to read the rest of its chapter with the verse highlighted. Below it are short summaries from public-domain commentaries, with links to the full text, and a place for your own comments. Tap an author's name for their books and a grid of verses from them, or use Search to jump to a reference like John 3:16.

The feed picks a chapter at random (all 1,189 are equally likely) and then a verse from it, so verses in short chapters come round a little more often than those in long ones. There is no feed ranking of any kind: nothing you do changes what you're shown.

Where the text comes from

  • Scripture is the Berean Standard Bible (BSB), which is in the public domain. The whole text is served with the site, so pages don't fetch it from anyone else.
  • Commentary summaries are excerpts of public-domain works: Matthew Henry, John Gill, Adam Clarke, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Keil & Delitzsch and the Tyndale Open Study Notes. They're fetched, when you open a post's comments, from the Free Use Bible API, with links out to Bible Hub, StudyLight and Enduring Word for more.
  • Authors and dates on posts are the traditional attributions (Moses for the Torah, David for most psalms, and so on), with rough traditional dates. Many books are anonymous and scholars disagree on several; treat them as a way in, not a verdict.

What is stored about you

Signed out, nothing leaves your browser. Your likes, saves, collections and comments are kept in this browser's storage as verse references (John 3:16, not the text), and the site has no accounts, analytics or tracking of any kind. Clearing the site's data removes them.

Signed in, your account holds exactly what the app needs and no more:

  • your email address, used only to send you sign-in codes; it is never shown to anyone;
  • a username and display name, which other signed-in people can find by searching;
  • your likes, saves, collections, Library and comments, as verse references with the time you added them, and your friendships.

Who sees what: your Library and your comments are visible to your friends (people who asked, or whom you asked, and the other accepted). Likes and your own collections are private. Friendships are visible only to the two people in them. These rules are enforced in the database itself, not just in the app.

There is no password. Signing in emails you a six-digit code. Delete account, in the account menu, removes your account and everything in it immediately.

Services involved

  • The site is served by Cloudflare. Its Turnstile check runs on the sign-in form, so the code-email endpoint can't be used to spam addresses; it normally needs nothing from you.
  • Accounts live in a Supabase project (a hosted Postgres database) that the app talks to directly from your browser. Like most sign-in systems, it keeps the IP address of each sign-in in its logs for a while.
  • Sign-in codes are emailed through Resend, which therefore handles your email address.
  • Fonts load from Google Fonts, and commentary from the Free Use Bible API, both of which see the request like any web server would.

Nothing is sold, shared for marketing, or used to build a profile of you.

Open source

Bibliofeed is open source under the MIT licence: github.com/SebastienHiggs/bibliofeed. The contributing page explains how to run it yourself and how changes are reviewed. Questions, mistakes and ideas are welcome as issues there.
